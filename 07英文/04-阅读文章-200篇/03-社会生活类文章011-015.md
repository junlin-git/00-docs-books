## 文章 11：The Science of Sleep

**难度**：B1-B2 | **字数**：约300词 | **主题**：睡眠科学

---

We spend roughly a third of our lives asleep. Yet despite how central sleep is to our existence, many of us know surprisingly little about why we need it — or the consequences of not getting enough.

Sleep is not a passive state of unconsciousness. It is a complex, active process during which the brain performs essential maintenance tasks. While we sleep, the brain consolidates memories — transferring information from short-term to long-term storage. It also clears out metabolic waste products that accumulate during waking hours, including proteins associated with Alzheimer's disease.

Sleep occurs in cycles of approximately 90 minutes, alternating between non-REM and REM (Rapid Eye Movement) sleep. Non-REM sleep, particularly the deep "slow-wave" stage, is when physical restoration occurs — tissue repair, muscle growth, and immune system strengthening. REM sleep, characterised by vivid dreaming, plays a key role in emotional regulation and creativity.

Adults require between seven and nine hours of sleep per night for optimal health. Chronic sleep deprivation — consistently sleeping less than six hours — has been linked to increased risk of obesity, heart disease, type 2 diabetes, and depression. Even a single night of poor sleep can impair judgement, reaction time, and emotional control to a degree comparable to mild alcohol intoxication.

Many modern sleep problems stem from behaviours that disrupt our natural circadian rhythms — the internal biological clock that regulates our sleep-wake cycle. Blue light emitted by screens suppresses melatonin production, delaying sleep onset. Caffeine, consumed too late in the day, can interfere with sleep quality for up to twelve hours.

The good news is that most sleep problems are addressable through behavioural changes: consistent sleep and wake times, a cool dark bedroom, limited screen time before bed, and avoiding caffeine after noon.

---

### 全文翻译

我们一生中大约有三分之一的时间在睡眠中度过。然而，尽管睡眠对我们的存在如此重要，我们许多人对为什么需要睡眠，以及睡眠不足会带来什么后果，了解得却出奇地少。

睡眠并不是一种被动的无意识状态。它是一个复杂而活跃的过程，在这个过程中，大脑会执行关键的维护任务。我们睡觉时，大脑会巩固记忆，把信息从短期储存转移到长期储存。它还会清除清醒时积累的代谢废物，其中包括与阿尔茨海默病相关的蛋白质。

睡眠以大约 90 分钟为一个周期，在非快速眼动睡眠和快速眼动睡眠之间交替。非快速眼动睡眠，尤其是深度的“慢波”阶段，是身体恢复发生的时候，包括组织修复、肌肉生长和免疫系统增强。快速眼动睡眠则以生动梦境为特征，在情绪调节和创造力方面发挥关键作用。

成年人每晚需要 7 到 9 小时睡眠才能达到最佳健康状态。长期睡眠不足，也就是持续每晚睡眠少于 6 小时，与肥胖、心脏病、2 型糖尿病和抑郁风险增加有关。即使只是一个晚上的睡眠不佳，也会损害判断力、反应时间和情绪控制，其程度可与轻度酒精中毒相当。

许多现代睡眠问题都源于打乱自然昼夜节律的行为。昼夜节律是调节睡眠与清醒周期的内部生物钟。屏幕发出的蓝光会抑制褪黑素分泌，从而推迟入睡。若在一天中过晚摄入咖啡因，它可能在长达 12 小时内干扰睡眠质量。

好消息是，大多数睡眠问题都可以通过行为改变来改善：保持固定的睡觉和起床时间，让卧室保持凉爽和黑暗，睡前减少屏幕使用，并且中午以后避免摄入咖啡因。

## 文章 12：The Mental Health Crisis Among Young People

**难度**：B2 | **字数**：约340词 | **主题**：青少年心理健康

---

Over the past decade, rates of anxiety, depression, and self-harm among young people in many countries have risen sharply. What was once considered a niche concern has become a public health emergency, prompting urgent questions about its causes.

The statistics are alarming. In the United Kingdom, one in six children between the ages of 5 and 16 is estimated to have a mental health problem. In the United States, the Surgeon General issued an unprecedented advisory in 2021 warning of a youth mental health crisis. Emergency department visits for self-harm among adolescents increased significantly in the years following the COVID-19 pandemic.

Researchers debate the causes. Social media is frequently cited as a contributing factor — research has found associations between heavy social media use and increased rates of anxiety and depression, particularly among teenage girls. The proposed mechanisms include cyberbullying, exposure to unrealistic beauty standards, and social comparison. However, many researchers caution against oversimplification — the relationship between social media and mental health is complex and not uniformly negative.

Academic pressure is another frequently mentioned factor. In many education systems, students face intense competition for university places and are subject to high-stakes examinations from an increasingly young age. The pandemic brought additional stressors — disrupted schooling, social isolation, and uncertainty about the future.

Economic anxiety also plays a role. Young people today face a more uncertain economic landscape than previous generations — housing costs have risen dramatically relative to wages, job markets are increasingly precarious, and climate change casts a long shadow over the future.

Addressing the crisis requires action on multiple fronts: reforming education systems to reduce excessive academic pressure, regulating social media platforms to protect young users, improving access to affordable mental health services, and fostering community and social connection. Ultimately, how a society treats its young people is a measure of its values and priorities.

---

### 全文翻译

在过去十年里，许多国家年轻人中的焦虑、抑郁和自我伤害比例急剧上升。曾经被视为小众问题的现象，如今已经变成公共卫生紧急事件，并引发了人们对其成因的迫切追问。

统计数据令人担忧。在英国，据估计每 6 名 5 至 16 岁儿童中就有 1 人存在心理健康问题。在美国，卫生局局长在 2021 年发布了一份前所未有的公告，警告青少年心理健康危机。新冠疫情后的几年里，青少年因自我伤害前往急诊科就诊的次数显著增加。

研究人员对原因存在争论。社交媒体经常被视为一个促成因素。研究发现，大量使用社交媒体与更高的焦虑和抑郁发生率相关，尤其是在少女群体中。被提出的机制包括网络欺凌、暴露在不切实际的审美标准之下，以及社会比较。然而，许多研究人员也提醒人们不要过度简化，因为社交媒体与心理健康之间的关系很复杂，而且并非完全负面。

学业压力是另一个经常被提及的因素。在许多教育体系中，学生为了争取大学名额面临激烈竞争，并且从越来越小的年纪起就要面对高压考试。疫情又带来了额外压力源，比如学业中断、社交隔离以及对未来的不确定感。

经济焦虑也起到了一定作用。与前几代人相比，如今的年轻人面临更不确定的经济环境。住房成本相对于工资大幅上升，就业市场越来越不稳定，而气候变化也给未来投下了长长的阴影。

应对这场危机需要多方面同时行动：改革教育体系以减轻过度学业压力，规范社交媒体平台以保护年轻用户，改善可负担心理健康服务的获取，并促进社区联系和社会联结。归根结底，一个社会如何对待年轻人，衡量的是它的价值观和优先事项。

## 文章 13：The Obesity Epidemic

**难度**：B1-B2 | **字数**：约320词 | **主题**：肥胖问题

---

Over the past half-century, the world has grown heavier. Global rates of obesity have tripled since 1975. Today, approximately 650 million adults worldwide are obese, and an additional 1.9 billion are overweight. Once considered a problem largely confined to wealthy Western nations, obesity is now a global challenge affecting countries at all income levels.

The causes are multiple and interrelated. At the most basic level, obesity results from an energy imbalance — consuming more calories than the body expends. However, this simple equation obscures a complex reality. The modern food environment makes it increasingly difficult to maintain a healthy weight. Ultra-processed foods — high in sugar, salt, and fat, engineered to be highly palatable and convenient — have become the dominant feature of diets in many countries. They are cheap, ubiquitous, and intensely marketed, particularly to children.

Physical activity has declined dramatically as economies have shifted towards sedentary work and car-dependent urban design. Many people now spend the majority of their waking hours sitting — at desks, in cars, or in front of screens.

Genetics also plays a role. Research suggests that susceptibility to weight gain is partly inherited, explaining why some people gain weight more easily than others in the same food environment.

The health consequences of obesity are severe. It is a major risk factor for type 2 diabetes, heart disease, stroke, several cancers, joint problems, and sleep apnoea. The economic costs are enormous — obesity-related healthcare costs are estimated at trillions of dollars globally.

Addressing obesity effectively requires looking beyond individual willpower. Public health approaches — taxing sugary drinks, regulating food marketing to children, redesigning urban spaces to encourage walking and cycling, and reforming food labelling — have shown promising results in several countries.

---

### 全文翻译

过去半个世纪里，全世界都变得更胖了。自 1975 年以来，全球肥胖率已经增长了三倍。如今，全世界大约有 6.5 亿成年人属于肥胖，另外还有 19 亿人超重。肥胖曾被认为主要是富裕西方国家的问题，但现在它已经成为影响所有收入水平国家的全球性挑战。

其成因多种多样且彼此关联。从最基本的层面看，肥胖源于能量失衡，也就是摄入的热量多于身体消耗的热量。然而，这个简单公式掩盖了复杂现实。现代食品环境使人们越来越难维持健康体重。超加工食品含糖、盐和脂肪高，并被设计得既美味又方便，已经成为许多国家饮食中的主导部分。它们价格便宜、无处不在，而且被大力营销，尤其是面向儿童。

随着经济向久坐型工作和依赖汽车的城市设计转变，体力活动大幅下降。如今很多人清醒的大部分时间都坐着度过，无论是在办公桌前、汽车里，还是在屏幕前。

遗传因素也会发挥作用。研究表明，个体对体重增加的易感性部分来自遗传，这解释了为什么在同样的食品环境中，有些人比另一些人更容易长胖。

肥胖带来的健康后果十分严重。它是 2 型糖尿病、心脏病、中风、多种癌症、关节问题和睡眠呼吸暂停的重要风险因素。其经济成本也极其惊人，据估计，全球与肥胖相关的医疗支出高达数万亿美元。

要有效应对肥胖，就必须把视线放到个人意志力之外。公共卫生方法，例如对含糖饮料征税、限制面向儿童的食品营销、重新设计城市空间以鼓励步行和骑行，以及改革食品标签制度，都已在一些国家显示出积极成效。

## 文章 14：The Benefits of Exercise Beyond Physical Fitness

**难度**：B2 | **字数**：约320词 | **主题**：运动的益处

---

Most people understand that regular exercise is good for physical health — it strengthens the cardiovascular system, builds muscle, controls weight, and reduces the risk of chronic disease. What is less widely appreciated is the profound effect exercise has on the brain.

When you exercise, your heart pumps more blood to the brain, delivering oxygen and nutrients that support healthy function. But more significantly, exercise triggers the release of a cascade of neurochemicals — including endorphins, serotonin, dopamine, and BDNF (Brain-Derived Neurotrophic Factor). BDNF, sometimes called "Miracle-Gro for the brain," stimulates the growth of new neurons and strengthens connections between existing ones, particularly in the hippocampus — the brain region central to learning and memory.

The mental health benefits of exercise are well-established. Multiple meta-analyses have found exercise to be as effective as antidepressants in treating mild to moderate depression. Regular physical activity reduces anxiety, improves sleep quality, and builds resilience to stress. Neuroscientists believe this is partly because exercise gives the body's stress response systems appropriate physical expression — exercising processes the stress hormones that anxiety otherwise leaves circulating in the bloodstream.

Exercise also appears to protect against cognitive decline with age. Studies of older adults who exercise regularly show they have larger hippocampal volumes, better memory performance, and lower rates of dementia compared to sedentary peers.

Even modest amounts of exercise produce measurable benefits. A 20-minute walk has been shown to improve mood and reduce stress hormones immediately afterwards. The research suggests it is not necessary to become an athlete — consistency matters more than intensity.

The challenge is motivation and habit formation. Research on behaviour change suggests that making exercise social, tying it to an existing routine, and choosing activities you enjoy dramatically increases the likelihood of sustained engagement.

---

### 全文翻译

大多数人都明白，规律运动有益于身体健康，它能增强心血管系统、增加肌肉、控制体重，并降低慢性疾病风险。较少被广泛认识到的是，运动对大脑也有深远影响。

当你运动时，心脏会把更多血液泵向大脑，输送支持健康功能所需的氧气和营养物质。但更重要的是，运动会触发一连串神经化学物质的释放，其中包括内啡肽、血清素、多巴胺和 BDNF（脑源性神经营养因子）。BDNF 有时被称为“大脑的 Miracle-Gro”，它会刺激新神经元生长，并加强已有神经元之间的连接，尤其是在海马体中，而海马体是学习和记忆的核心脑区。

运动对心理健康的益处已经得到充分证实。多项荟萃分析发现，在治疗轻度到中度抑郁时，运动的效果可与抗抑郁药相当。规律体育活动能够减轻焦虑、提升睡眠质量，并增强对压力的韧性。神经科学家认为，这部分原因在于，运动让身体的压力反应系统得到了合适的生理表达，也就是通过运动处理掉那些若因焦虑而滞留在血液中的压力激素。

运动似乎还能预防随年龄增长而出现的认知衰退。针对经常运动的老年人的研究显示，与久坐同龄人相比，他们的海马体体积更大、记忆表现更好，而且痴呆发生率更低。

即使适量运动，也会带来可测量的益处。研究表明，散步 20 分钟后，人们的情绪会改善，压力激素会立刻下降。研究提示，没有必要成为运动员，一致性比强度更重要。

真正的挑战在于动机和习惯养成。有关行为改变的研究表明，让运动具有社交性、把它绑定到既有日常惯例上，以及选择自己喜欢的活动，都会显著提高长期坚持的可能性。

## 文章 15：The Revolution in Online Education

**难度**：B1-B2 | **字数**：约310词 | **主题**：在线教育

---

A decade ago, if you wanted to learn from a professor at Harvard, MIT, or Oxford, you needed to gain admission to one of those institutions. Today, millions of people around the world can access courses from these universities for free, from their own homes, through online learning platforms.

The rise of Massive Open Online Courses (MOOCs) — platforms like Coursera, edX, and Khan Academy — has democratised access to quality education in ways previously unimaginable. A student in rural Kenya can study computer science with the same materials used at Stanford. A middle-aged career changer can learn data science to update their skills without leaving their job. A curious retiree can audit philosophy courses from world-leading universities purely for personal enrichment.

The COVID-19 pandemic dramatically accelerated this shift. With physical campuses closed, schools and universities worldwide scrambled to move teaching online, rapidly expanding both the technology and the cultural acceptance of digital learning.

Yet online education is not without its limitations. Completion rates for MOOCs are notoriously low — often less than 10%. Without the structure, accountability, and social environment of a physical classroom, many students struggle to maintain motivation over time. The hands-on learning required in subjects like laboratory science, medicine, or practical arts cannot easily be replicated online.

There are also equity concerns. Online learning assumes reliable internet access and a quiet, suitable study space — conditions that are not universally available. Students from disadvantaged backgrounds are often less well-served by online approaches.

The future of education is likely not purely online, but hybrid — combining the global reach and flexibility of digital platforms with the mentorship, collaboration, and community of in-person learning. The pandemic accelerated a shift that was already underway; the challenge now is to realise its potential equitably.

---

### 全文翻译

十年前，如果你想向哈佛、麻省理工或牛津的教授学习，你必须先被这些学校录取。如今，世界各地的数百万人都可以通过在线学习平台，在自己家中免费学习这些大学的课程。

大规模开放式在线课程，也就是 MOOCs，诸如 Coursera、edX 和 Khan Academy 这样的平台，以前所未有的方式让优质教育变得更加普及。肯尼亚偏远地区的一名学生，可以使用与斯坦福相同的材料学习计算机科学。一名中年转行者可以在不辞职的情况下学习数据科学来更新技能。一位好奇的退休人士则可以纯粹出于个人兴趣旁听世界顶尖大学的哲学课程。

新冠疫情极大加速了这一转变。随着实体校园关闭，全球各地学校和大学仓促将教学转到线上，这迅速推动了技术发展，也提升了人们对数字学习的文化接受度。

然而，在线教育并非没有局限。MOOC 的完成率一直都很低，常常不到 10%。如果没有实体课堂所提供的结构、监督和社交环境，许多学生很难长期保持学习动力。像实验科学、医学或实践艺术这类学科所要求的动手学习，也很难在网上轻易复制。

此外，这里面还有公平性问题。在线学习默认人们拥有可靠的网络连接和安静、合适的学习空间，但这些条件并非人人都具备。来自弱势背景的学生往往无法从线上模式中同样受益。

教育的未来很可能不是纯线上，而是混合式的，把数字平台的全球覆盖和灵活性，与线下学习中的指导、合作和社区感结合起来。疫情加速了本已开始的转变；现在的挑战，是让这种潜力以更公平的方式实现。
