## 文章 41：Climate Change: The Science and the Stakes

**难度**：B2-C1 | **字数**：约380词 | **主题**：气候变化科学

---

The scientific case for human-caused climate change is now overwhelming. Since the industrial revolution, human activities — primarily the burning of fossil fuels — have released enormous quantities of carbon dioxide and other greenhouse gases into the atmosphere, enhancing the natural greenhouse effect and causing the planet's average temperature to rise.

The Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC), the world's leading body for climate science, has concluded that global average temperatures have already risen approximately 1.1°C above pre-industrial levels. The consequences are already visible: rising sea levels, more frequent and intense extreme weather events, retreating glaciers, changes in precipitation patterns, and shifting ecosystems.

The critical threshold that climate scientists have warned must not be crossed is 1.5°C of warming above pre-industrial levels — the target set by the 2015 Paris Agreement. Beyond this threshold, the risks of irreversible tipping points — events that could trigger self-reinforcing chains of warming — increase dramatically. Among the most feared are the melting of Arctic permafrost (releasing stored methane, a powerful greenhouse gas), the collapse of the West Antarctic ice sheet, and the die-off of the Amazon rainforest.

The challenge is fundamentally one of human systems rather than natural ones. The physics of the greenhouse effect has been understood since the 19th century. The emissions that are causing warming come from the energy systems, agricultural practices, transport networks, and industrial processes that underpin modern civilisation. Transforming these systems at the necessary speed is one of the greatest challenges humanity has ever faced.

Responses are emerging on multiple fronts. Renewable energy — particularly solar and wind — has grown dramatically and is now often the cheapest source of new electricity generation in most of the world. Electric vehicles are replacing combustion engines. Deforestation rates are falling in some regions. Carbon capture technologies are being developed.

Yet the gap between current policies and the trajectories needed to meet the Paris Agreement targets remains large. The IPCC has been clear: avoiding the worst outcomes of climate change requires rapid, deep, and immediate reductions in emissions across all sectors of the global economy.

## 文章 42：The Sixth Mass Extinction

**难度**：B2-C1 | **字数**：约340词 | **主题**：物种灭绝

---

In the 4.5 billion years of Earth's history, there have been five mass extinction events — episodes during which a large percentage of all species on Earth disappeared in a geologically brief period of time. Scientists now warn that we are in the early stages of a sixth mass extinction — one caused not by an asteroid impact or volcanic super-eruption, but by human activity.

The current rate of species extinction is estimated to be between 100 and 1,000 times the natural "background" extinction rate — the rate at which species would naturally disappear in the absence of human influence. Species are disappearing so rapidly that scientists estimate we are losing biodiversity at a rate unprecedented in millions of years.

The primary drivers of this crisis are well-documented. Habitat destruction — particularly deforestation, agricultural expansion, and urbanisation — has eliminated or degraded the ecosystems on which countless species depend. Pollution has poisoned water and soil systems. Climate change is altering the conditions to which species have adapted over millennia. Invasive species, introduced deliberately or accidentally, often devastate native ecosystems. Overexploitation — through hunting, fishing, and the wildlife trade — directly depletes populations.

The consequences extend beyond the intrinsic value of biodiversity. Ecosystems provide essential services on which human civilisation depends: clean water, air purification, pollination of crops, soil formation, and natural climate regulation. As biodiversity declines, these services are undermined.

The critically endangered status of charismatic species — tigers, elephants, blue whales, gorillas — has generated public concern and conservation action. But the crisis extends far beyond these visible animals. Insects, which underpin almost all terrestrial food chains and are responsible for most plant pollination, have declined by an estimated 75% in many regions over the past 25 years.

Conservation efforts are expanding — protected areas, captive breeding programmes, restoration of degraded habitats — but the scale of action required is far greater than what is currently being achieved. Protecting biodiversity requires transforming the economic systems and consumption patterns that drive habitat destruction.

## 文章 43：The Ocean: Earth's Life Support System

**难度**：B2 | **字数**：约330词 | **主题**：海洋生态系统

---

The ocean covers 71% of Earth's surface and contains 97% of the planet's water. It regulates climate, produces oxygen, absorbs carbon dioxide, feeds billions of people, and drives the global water cycle. Yet despite its fundamental importance to all life on Earth, the ocean remains poorly understood and dramatically under-protected.

The ocean's role in regulating the planet's climate is immense. It absorbs approximately 90% of the excess heat generated by greenhouse gas emissions, dramatically slowing the pace of atmospheric warming. It also absorbs around 25-30% of the carbon dioxide humans produce, performing a service whose economic value is incalculable.

This service comes at a cost. As oceans absorb more carbon dioxide, they become more acidic — a process called ocean acidification. The ocean's pH has already decreased by 0.1 units since the industrial revolution, representing a 26% increase in acidity. This is profoundly damaging to marine organisms that build calcium carbonate shells and skeletons — from coral reefs to oysters to tiny plankton that form the base of oceanic food chains.

Coral reefs — often called the "rainforests of the sea" — support approximately 25% of all marine species despite covering less than 0.1% of the ocean floor. Rising ocean temperatures are causing mass coral bleaching events: when water becomes too warm, corals expel the symbiotic algae that give them colour and nutrients, turning white and often dying. The Great Barrier Reef has experienced several unprecedented mass bleaching events since 2016.

Plastic pollution presents another major threat. An estimated 8 million tonnes of plastic enter the ocean every year. This plastic persists for centuries, breaking down into microplastics that are now found throughout the ocean — from the surface to the deepest trenches — and are entering marine food chains.

Protecting the ocean requires ambitious international agreement: expanding marine protected areas, reducing carbon emissions, phasing out harmful fishing practices, and dramatically cutting plastic production.

## 文章 44：The Amazon Rainforest: Earth's Lungs

**难度**：B2 | **字数**：约320词 | **主题**：亚马逊雨林

---

Stretching across nine countries and covering an area roughly equivalent to the contiguous United States, the Amazon rainforest is the largest tropical rainforest on Earth. It is home to an estimated 10% of all species on the planet, contains more freshwater than any other river system, and plays a critical role in regulating South America's climate and the global carbon cycle.

The Amazon stores an estimated 150-200 billion tonnes of carbon in its biomass — equivalent to several decades of current global carbon emissions. It also recycles enormous quantities of water, releasing moisture into the atmosphere that falls as rainfall across the continent. Scientists estimate that the Amazon "flies" around 20 billion tonnes of water into the atmosphere every day through evapotranspiration — more than the flow of the Amazon River itself.

Deforestation is the primary threat to this irreplaceable ecosystem. Since the 1970s, approximately 20% of the Amazon has been cleared — primarily for cattle ranching and soya production. At current rates, scientists estimate the Amazon could reach a "tipping point" at which degradation becomes self-reinforcing: the forest begins to dry out, can no longer sustain itself, and transitions into a savannah-like ecosystem — releasing its stored carbon and devastating regional rainfall patterns.

Scientists estimate this tipping point could be reached at between 20-25% deforestation of the original forest extent. Given that approximately 20% has already been destroyed and further degradation from fires and climate stress is ongoing, the timeline for action is extremely short.

International attention to Amazon deforestation has fluctuated significantly with changes in Brazilian government policy. Under President Bolsonaro (2019-2022), deforestation accelerated to record levels, prompting international outcry. His successor has pledged to end deforestation by 2030.

Saving the Amazon requires addressing the economic incentives that drive deforestation — rewarding forest stewardship rather than penalising it.

## 文章 45：Renewable Energy: The Race to Power the Future

**难度**：B2 | **字数**：约330词 | **主题**：可再生能源

---

In 2023, for the first time, renewable energy accounted for more than a third of global electricity generation. Solar power capacity alone grew by 50% in a single year. The transition to clean energy is no longer a distant aspiration — it is happening, faster than almost anyone predicted a decade ago.

The primary drivers of this transformation are falling costs and improving technology. The cost of solar photovoltaic (PV) electricity has fallen by approximately 99% since 1976 and by 90% in just the last decade — a rate of cost reduction with few parallels in economic history. Onshore wind power has followed a similar trajectory. In most countries, new renewable electricity is now significantly cheaper than new fossil fuel generation.

Wind and solar power are inherently variable — they generate electricity only when the wind blows or the sun shines. Managing this variability is the central technical challenge of the energy transition. Solutions include long-distance transmission grids that can balance supply and demand across large areas, demand response systems that shift electricity consumption to times of high generation, and energy storage — particularly batteries.

Battery technology has also improved dramatically and declined sharply in cost. The same technological learning curve that transformed solar power is now playing out for grid-scale and vehicle batteries. As battery costs fall further, the economics of energy storage improve, making higher proportions of variable renewable energy viable.

Hydrogen — produced using renewable electricity through electrolysis — offers another storage medium and could potentially decarbonise sectors where direct electrification is difficult, such as heavy industry and long-distance shipping.

The pace of the energy transition will be determined partly by markets and partly by policy — carbon pricing, clean energy standards, regulations on fossil fuel use, and public investment in infrastructure. The technology exists; what is required is the political will to deploy it at the necessary speed.
