## 文章 91：The Pressure to Be Productive All the Time

**难度**：B2 | **字数**：约240词 | **主题**：持续高效压力

---

In many modern workplaces and online communities, productivity is treated not just as a useful habit but as a moral value. People are encouraged to wake up early, optimize every hour, track their habits, and turn hobbies into side businesses. Social media is full of advice about "winning the morning," "using dead time," and "staying ahead." Rest, by contrast, is often described as something that must be earned.

This mindset can be motivating in small doses. Planning well and using time intentionally can help people reach important goals. But when productivity becomes the main measure of personal worth, it easily turns unhealthy. People may feel guilty for relaxing, meeting friends without a purpose, or spending time on activities that do not produce visible results. Even sleep, exercise, and leisure may be valued only because they improve later performance.

The constant pressure to optimize says something troubling about contemporary life. In insecure economies, many people feel they must always do more just to avoid falling behind. Technology makes that feeling stronger by allowing work, messages, and self-comparison to continue at all hours. A healthier view would treat productivity as a tool, not an identity. Human beings need unstructured time, play, and idleness as well as achievement. A society that forgets this may produce efficient workers, but it will struggle to produce balanced lives.

### 全文翻译

在许多现代职场和网络社群中，效率不再只是一个有用习惯，而几乎被当成一种道德价值。人们被鼓励早起、优化每个小时、记录习惯，并把爱好变成副业。社交媒体上充满了关于“赢在清晨”“利用碎片时间”“始终领先一步”的建议。相比之下，休息常常被描述成一种必须先挣到的东西。

这种心态在少量情况下确实能带来激励。合理规划、主动使用时间，能够帮助人们实现重要目标。但当生产力变成衡量个人价值的主要标准时，它很容易走向不健康。人们可能会为放松、为没有明确目的地和朋友见面、或把时间花在不会产出可见结果的事情上而感到内疚。甚至睡眠、运动和休闲，也可能只因为能提高之后的表现而被认可。

这种持续优化的压力揭示了当代生活中令人不安的一面。在不稳定的经济环境中，许多人觉得自己必须不断做得更多，才能避免落后。技术又让这种感觉更强，因为工作、消息和自我比较可以在任何时刻继续。更健康的看法应该把效率当成工具，而不是身份本身。人类除了成就，也需要无结构的时间、玩乐和无所事事。一个忘记这一点的社会，也许能制造高效的劳动者，却很难培养平衡的人生。

## 文章 92：The Social Meaning of Remote Work

**难度**：B1-B2 | **字数**：约235词 | **主题**：远程办公的社会影响

---

Remote work is often discussed in terms of convenience, cost, and technology, but it has social consequences too. For many employees, working from home removes the daily commute, offers more control over time, and makes it easier to manage childcare or other responsibilities. Some people report higher concentration without the noise and interruptions of an office. For workers with disabilities or long travel times, remote work can be especially valuable.

At the same time, the office was never only a place for tasks. It was also a place where people formed weak but meaningful social ties. Casual conversations before meetings, shared lunches, and small acts of mutual help created a sense of belonging that is hard to reproduce online. New employees may find it especially difficult to learn workplace culture through scheduled video calls alone.

The debate about remote work is therefore not simply about where people sit with a laptop. It is about what kind of social world work should create. A fully remote model may increase freedom for some, while making others feel isolated or invisible. A full return to the office may rebuild connection, but it can also ignore the unequal burdens of commuting and caregiving. The most workable future may be one that treats flexibility and human connection as equally important rather than forcing workers to choose one against the other.

### 全文翻译

远程办公通常从便利、成本和技术角度被讨论，但它也有社会后果。对许多员工来说，在家工作减少了通勤，给予他们更多时间控制权，也更容易兼顾育儿或其他责任。有些人表示，在没有办公室噪音和频繁打断的情况下，他们反而更容易专注。对于残障员工或通勤时间很长的人来说，远程办公尤其有价值。

但办公室从来不只是一个完成任务的地方。它也是人们形成“弱连接”却又有意义的社会关系的场所。会前的闲聊、一起吃午饭，以及小小的互相帮忙，都能带来一种归属感，而这种感觉很难完全通过线上复制。新员工尤其容易发现，仅靠安排好的视频会议，很难真正学会工作场所的文化。

因此，关于远程办公的争论，不仅仅是人们带着笔记本坐在哪里的问题。它其实是在讨论，工作应当创造怎样的社会世界。完全远程可能给一部分人带来更大自由，同时也让另一部分人感到孤立或被忽视。全面回到办公室或许能重建联系，但也可能无视通勤和照护责任的不平等负担。最可行的未来，也许是把灵活性和人与人之间的联结都视为同样重要，而不是迫使员工在两者之间二选一。

## 文章 93：Why Young Adults Delay Major Life Decisions

**难度**：B1-B2 | **字数**：约240词 | **主题**：年轻人推迟人生决定

---

Across many countries, young adults are marrying later, buying homes later, and having children later than previous generations. Some commentators interpret this as selfishness or fear of commitment. The reality is usually more complicated. Housing is expensive, secure jobs are harder to find, and student debt weighs heavily on many people in their twenties and thirties. Under such conditions, decisions that once seemed like natural steps can feel financially risky.

Cultural expectations have changed as well. Earlier generations often followed a more fixed path from education to work to marriage and family. Today, people are encouraged to explore identity, move between jobs, and seek personal fulfillment before settling down. This can create freedom, but it can also create uncertainty. When there are many possible paths and no clear social timetable, choosing one becomes more difficult.

Delaying major decisions is therefore not always a sign of immaturity. In many cases, it reflects adaptation to a more unstable world. Still, there are emotional costs. People may feel stuck between wanting stability and fearing the burden it brings. They may also face judgment from older relatives who lived under very different conditions. Understanding this shift requires more than nostalgia. It requires seeing how economics, culture, and personal aspiration now interact in new ways.

### 全文翻译

在许多国家，年轻人结婚更晚、买房更晚、生孩子也更晚，相比前几代明显推迟了。一些评论者把这解释为自私或害怕承诺，但现实通常更复杂。住房昂贵、稳定工作更难获得，学生贷款也让许多二三十岁的人背负沉重压力。在这样的条件下，那些曾经被看作自然人生步骤的决定，如今会显得财务风险很高。

文化期待也发生了变化。更早一代人往往遵循一条相对固定的路线：教育、工作、婚姻、家庭。今天，人们则被鼓励先探索自我、在不同工作间转换，并在安定下来前追求个人满足。这种变化带来了自由，但也制造了不确定性。当可能的道路很多，却没有清晰的社会时间表时，做选择反而更难。

因此，推迟重大人生决定，并不总是不成熟的表现。在很多情况下，这只是对更不稳定世界的一种适应。不过，这也有情感代价。人们可能一方面渴望稳定，另一方面又害怕它带来的负担。他们还可能面对来自年长亲属的评判，而这些长辈生活在完全不同的条件下。理解这种变化，不能只靠怀旧，而要看到经济、文化和个人理想如今是如何以新的方式相互作用的。

## 文章 94：Digital Minimalism in Daily Life

**难度**：B1-B2 | **字数**：约235词 | **主题**：数字极简主义

---

After years of adding more apps, notifications, and devices to daily life, some people are moving in the opposite direction. They are deleting social media, turning phones grayscale, limiting messages, or keeping certain hours completely screen-free. This approach is often called digital minimalism. It does not reject technology entirely. Instead, it asks people to use digital tools more deliberately and to remove those that bring more distraction than value.

The appeal of digital minimalism is easy to understand. Many people feel mentally crowded by constant alerts, endless scrolling, and the pressure to respond immediately. A phone may save time in one moment and waste an hour in the next. By reducing digital noise, people hope to recover attention for reading, sleep, conversation, and work that requires concentration. Some also report a stronger sense of calm when they are no longer checking updates every few minutes.

However, practicing digital minimalism is not always simple. Schools, employers, friends, and families often expect fast online replies. Important services are increasingly organized through apps. Stepping back from constant connection can therefore feel socially costly. The goal, then, is not purity but balance. Digital minimalism matters because it raises a basic question: are we using technology to support our lives, or arranging our lives around technology? For many people, asking that question is already a meaningful first step.

### 全文翻译

在经历了多年不断给生活加上更多应用、提醒和设备之后，一些人开始朝相反方向移动。他们删除社交媒体，把手机调成灰阶，限制消息提醒，或者规定某些时段完全不看屏幕。这种做法常被称为数字极简主义。它并不是完全拒绝技术，而是要求人们更有意识地使用数字工具，并把那些带来干扰多于价值的部分移除。

数字极简主义的吸引力并不难理解。许多人都感觉自己的精神空间被持续提醒、无尽刷屏和必须立刻回应的压力挤满了。手机可能在某一刻帮你省时间，却又在下一刻浪费掉整整一小时。通过减少数字噪音，人们希望把注意力重新拿回来，用于阅读、睡眠、交流以及需要专注的工作。一些人也表示，当自己不再每几分钟就查看一次更新时，会更平静。

不过，实践数字极简主义并不总是简单。学校、雇主、朋友和家人往往期待你快速在线回应。许多重要服务也越来越依赖应用程序完成。因此，从持续在线中退后一步，可能会带来社交上的代价。于是，它的目标不是纯粹，而是平衡。数字极简主义之所以重要，是因为它提出了一个基础问题：我们是在用技术支持生活，还是在围着技术安排生活？对许多人来说，仅仅提出这个问题，就已经是有意义的第一步。

## 文章 95：The Revival of Repair Culture

**难度**：B1-B2 | **字数**：约240词 | **主题**：修理文化回归

---

For many years, modern consumer culture encouraged replacement rather than repair. When a toaster stopped working or a phone battery weakened, people were expected to buy a newer model. Recently, that attitude has started to change. Repair cafes, community workshops, and online tutorial groups are helping people fix clothing, appliances, bicycles, and small electronics instead of throwing them away. In some countries, the "right to repair" has also become a public policy issue.

This revival is driven by several concerns. Cost is one reason: replacing everyday items can be expensive. Environmental pressure is another. Constant disposal creates waste and uses raw materials that are difficult to recover. But repair culture is not only about money or sustainability. It also changes the relationship between people and objects. Repair requires patience, attention, and basic practical knowledge. It turns users back into participants rather than passive consumers.

There is a social side as well. Community repair events bring strangers together around shared problems and shared skills. Older people may teach younger ones how to sew, solder, or maintain tools. Volunteers help others extend the life of devices they could not otherwise afford to replace. Repair culture therefore offers more than technical usefulness. It suggests a different social logic, one based on care, continuity, and responsibility. In a throwaway economy, that shift is significant.

### 全文翻译

很多年来，现代消费文化鼓励的是换新而不是修理。当烤面包机坏了，或者手机电池变差时，人们被默认应该直接买更新的型号。最近，这种态度开始发生变化。修理咖啡馆、社区工作坊和线上教程群组正在帮助人们修补衣物、家电、自行车和小型电子产品，而不是把它们扔掉。在一些国家，维修权甚至已经成为公共政策议题。

这种复兴由多种担忧推动。成本是一个原因，更换日常物品可能很贵。环境压力是另一个原因。不断丢弃会制造废物，也会消耗难以回收的原材料。但修理文化并不只关乎金钱或可持续性。它还改变了人与物之间的关系。修理需要耐心、专注和基本的实践知识，它让使用者重新变成参与者，而不只是被动消费者。

它还有明显的社会面向。社区修理活动会围绕共同问题和共同技能，把陌生人聚在一起。年长者可以教年轻人缝补、焊接或维护工具。志愿者帮助别人延长设备寿命，让他们不必承担无法负担的更换费用。因此，修理文化提供的不只是技术上的实用性，它还暗示着另一套社会逻辑，一套建立在照料、延续和责任之上的逻辑。在一个一次性经济占主导的时代，这种转变很重要。
