## 文章 96：Pet Ownership and Urban Emotional Support

**难度**：B1-B2 | **字数**：约235词 | **主题**：城市养宠与情感支持

---

In large cities, pets are often described as family members rather than animals that simply share a home. Dogs and cats provide companionship in places where many adults live alone, work long hours, or feel disconnected from neighbors. Walking a dog can create routine, encourage exercise, and lead to small conversations with other pet owners. For some people, caring for an animal offers emotional stability during periods of stress, grief, or change.

The growing importance of pets also reflects changes in urban life. Marriage is later, family size is smaller, and many people delay parenthood or decide not to have children. Under these conditions, pets can become central sources of affection and daily structure. The pet industry has responded with daycare centers, grooming salons, pet insurance, and even birthday parties. This shows how deeply animals have entered modern social life.

Yet pet ownership also highlights inequality and responsibility. Veterinary care, housing deposits, and daily time commitments can be costly. Many renters struggle to find pet-friendly homes. During economic hardship, some owners face painful choices about whether they can continue caring for an animal well. Pets give comfort, but they also depend completely on human commitment. Their role in city life reminds us that companionship is valuable, but real care always involves labor, money, and sacrifice.

### 全文翻译

在大城市里，宠物常常被当作家庭成员，而不只是住在同一屋檐下的动物。对那些独居、工作时间长或与邻里缺少联系的成年人来说，狗和猫提供了陪伴。遛狗能带来日常节奏、促进运动，也会引出与其他养宠人士之间的短暂交流。对一些人而言，在压力、悲伤或生活变化时期，照顾动物还能提供情绪上的稳定感。

宠物的重要性上升，也反映了城市生活的变化。人们结婚更晚，家庭规模更小，很多人延迟生育，或者决定不生孩子。在这种情况下，宠物就可能成为主要的情感来源和日常生活结构的一部分。宠物行业也随之回应，出现了日托中心、美容店、宠物保险，甚至宠物生日派对。这说明动物已经多么深入地进入现代社会生活。

然而，养宠物也突出了不平等与责任问题。兽医费用、住房押金以及每日投入的时间都可能很高。许多租房者很难找到允许养宠物的住所。在经济困难时期，一些主人甚至要面对是否还能继续好好照顾动物的痛苦选择。宠物带来安慰，但它们也完全依赖人类的承诺。它们在城市生活中的角色提醒我们，陪伴很珍贵，但真正的照料始终伴随着劳动、金钱和牺牲。

## 文章 97：The Culture of Rating Everything

**难度**：B2 | **字数**：约240词 | **主题**：评分文化

---

Today, many ordinary experiences end with a request for evaluation. After a taxi ride, food delivery, online purchase, medical appointment, or hotel stay, people are asked to give stars, scores, or short reviews. Ratings can be useful. They help customers compare services quickly and can reward businesses that treat people well. For workers and small companies, good reviews may bring important visibility in a crowded market.

However, the habit of rating everything also changes how social life feels. It turns more and more interactions into performances that may later be judged. A driver, teacher, freelancer, or restaurant worker may feel that one difficult customer can damage their reputation immediately. Customers, meanwhile, are pushed to think like constant evaluators rather than participants in ordinary human exchanges. Not every experience needs to be scored to have value.

The rating culture reflects a wider desire to make life measurable and predictable. Numbers seem objective, efficient, and easy to compare. But they often hide context. A low score may come from bad weather, a delayed train, or a misunderstanding unrelated to the worker's effort. Over time, constant evaluation can create anxiety on both sides. Ratings are not going away, but society may need clearer limits on when they help and when they reduce complex human relations to misleading metrics.

### 全文翻译

如今，许多普通经历的结尾都会跟着一个请评价的请求。无论是打车、外卖、网购、就医还是住酒店，人们都会被要求给出星级、分数或简短评论。评分当然有其作用。它帮助顾客快速比较服务，也能奖励那些真正善待顾客的商家。对劳动者和小企业而言，好评还可能带来重要的曝光机会。

然而，什么都要打分的习惯，也在改变社会生活的感觉。它让越来越多的互动都像是一场可能被事后评判的表演。司机、教师、自由职业者或餐厅服务员，可能会担心一个难缠顾客就会立刻损害自己的声誉。与此同时，顾客也被推着去以持续评审者的方式思考，而不是以普通参与者的方式经历日常交往。并不是每段体验都必须被量化，才算有价值。

评分文化反映了一种更广泛的愿望，那就是让生活变得可测量、可预测。数字看起来客观、高效，也方便比较。但它们常常掩盖背景。一个低分，也许源于坏天气、晚点的火车，或者与劳动者努力无关的误会。久而久之，持续评价会在双方身上都制造焦虑。评分不会消失，但社会也许需要更清晰的边界，去判断它何时真正有帮助，何时又把复杂的人际关系压缩成误导性的指标。

## 文章 98：Shared Bicycles and Public Trust

**难度**：B1-B2 | **字数**：约230词 | **主题**：共享单车与公共信任

---

Bike-sharing programs have spread through cities around the world as governments look for cleaner and more flexible transport. For users, shared bicycles offer a practical option for short trips between bus stops, offices, stations, and homes. They reduce the need to own a bike, store it safely, or maintain it regularly. When combined with public transport, they can make urban movement faster and less dependent on cars.

Yet bike sharing depends on more than technology. It also depends on public trust and everyday cooperation. Users must return bikes responsibly. Cities must provide safe lanes, parking areas, and basic maintenance systems. If bikes are left in blocked entrances, thrown into rivers, or damaged without consequence, the whole system begins to fail. The success of shared transport is therefore partly a question of civic behavior, not just app design.

This is why bike-sharing programs are socially interesting. They show whether people believe public goods should be protected even when no one personally owns them. In places where the system works well, it can strengthen a sense that the city belongs to everyone. In places where it fails, the problem is often described as technical, but it may actually reflect weak trust and weak accountability. A shared bicycle is a simple object, yet it reveals a great deal about how urban communities function.

### 全文翻译

随着政府寻找更清洁、更灵活的交通方式，共享单车项目已经扩展到世界各地的城市。对用户来说，共享自行车为公交站、办公室、车站和住宅之间的短途出行提供了实用选择。它减少了个人拥有自行车、妥善存放和定期维护的需要。当它与公共交通结合时，还能让城市出行更快、更少依赖汽车。

然而，共享单车依赖的不只是技术，也依赖公共信任和日常合作。用户必须负责任地归还车辆。城市则要提供安全车道、停车区域和基本维护系统。如果单车被随意扔在门口、丢进河里，或者在损坏后无人承担后果，整个系统就会开始失灵。因此，共享交通是否成功，在一定程度上是一个公民行为问题，而不只是应用设计问题。

这也正是共享单车在社会层面上有意思的地方。它能显示出，人们是否相信公共物品即使不归个人所有，也值得被保护。在运行良好的地方，它能增强一种城市属于所有人的感觉。在失败的地方，问题往往被描述成技术缺陷，但真正反映的可能是信任薄弱和责任感薄弱。共享单车只是一个简单物件，却能揭示很多关于城市社区如何运作的事实。

## 文章 99：Short Videos and Family Communication

**难度**：B1-B2 | **字数**：约235词 | **主题**：短视频与家庭沟通

---

Short video platforms are usually associated with entertainment, trends, and young users, but they have also become part of family communication. In many households, parents, grandparents, and adult children send one another short clips throughout the day. Some are funny, some informative, and some are simply a way to say, "I thought of you." For relatives living in different cities or countries, these quick exchanges can create a light but steady sense of connection.

Videos sometimes cross generations more easily than text. A grandparent who rarely writes long messages may still enjoy sending a recipe clip or a traditional song. Younger relatives may share travel scenes, pet videos, or practical advice in a format that feels immediate and easy to understand. In this way, short videos can reduce distance and create new shared references within families.

At the same time, this form of communication has limits. Not every forwarded clip is accurate, and family group chats can spread health rumors or misleading news very quickly. Some people also feel overwhelmed by the volume of content. The value of short videos therefore depends on how they are used. They are strongest when they support real relationships instead of replacing them. Like many digital habits, they can either deepen communication or turn it into background noise.

### 全文翻译

短视频平台通常让人联想到娱乐、潮流和年轻用户，但它们也已经成为家庭沟通的一部分。在许多家庭里，父母、祖父母和成年子女会在一天中互相发送短视频。有些是好笑的，有些是有信息量的，还有一些只是表达“我想到你了”的一种方式。对住在不同城市甚至不同国家的亲人来说，这种快速交换能够带来一种轻但稳定的联系感。

视频有时比文字更容易跨越代际。一位很少发长文字消息的祖父母，也许仍然乐于分享一道菜谱视频或一首老歌。年轻亲属则可能分享旅行画面、宠物短片，或以更直接、更容易理解的方式发送实用建议。从这个意义上说，短视频可以缩短距离，并在家庭内部创造新的共同话题。

与此同时，这种沟通方式也有局限。并不是每一个被转发的短片都准确可靠，家庭群聊也可能很快传播健康谣言或误导信息。有些人还会被内容数量压得喘不过气。因此，短视频的价值取决于人们如何使用它们。它最有效的时候，是在支持真实关系，而不是取代真实关系。像许多数字习惯一样，它既可能加深沟通，也可能让沟通退化成背景噪音。

## 文章 100：The New Importance of Boundaries After Work

**难度**：B2 | **字数**：约235词 | **主题**：下班后的边界感

---

Smartphones and laptops have made work more flexible, but they have also made it harder to know when work ends. Messages arrive at dinner, emails appear during weekends, and tasks that once stayed in the office now follow people into bedrooms and holidays. In some industries, being constantly available is treated as a sign of dedication. Yet more workers are beginning to question that expectation.

The demand for boundaries is not only about comfort. It is also about mental health, family life, and long-term effectiveness. When people never fully disconnect, stress can remain high and recovery stays incomplete. Parents may be physically present but mentally occupied. Even employees without children need time in which their thoughts are not organized around deadlines and requests. Rest is not the opposite of responsible work; it is one condition that makes responsible work possible.

Different countries and companies are responding in different ways. Some have introduced rules limiting after-hours contact. Others encourage employees to turn off notifications or avoid sending non-urgent messages at night. Cultural change is slower than policy change, however. Real boundaries depend on whether managers respect them and whether workers feel safe using them. The debate matters because it asks a basic question about modern life: should technology expand freedom, or simply extend working time?

### 全文翻译

智能手机和笔记本电脑让工作变得更灵活，但也让人更难判断工作何时真正结束。晚餐时会收到消息，周末会跳出邮件，过去留在办公室的任务，如今会跟着人回到卧室和假期里。在一些行业中，时刻在线甚至被当成投入和敬业的标志。然而，越来越多的劳动者开始质疑这种期待。

人们要求边界感，并不只是为了舒适。它还关系到心理健康、家庭生活和长期效率。当一个人从不真正断开连接时，压力就会始终维持在高位，恢复也无法完整发生。父母可能身体在家，却在精神上一直被工作占据。即使没有孩子的员工，也需要一些时刻，让自己的思考不被截止日期和请求组织起来。休息不是认真工作的反面，它恰恰是认真工作得以成立的条件之一。

不同国家和公司正在用不同方式回应。有些地方已经推出限制下班后联系的规则。另一些地方则鼓励员工关闭提醒，或者避免在夜间发送非紧急消息。不过，文化变化总比制度变化慢。真正的边界取决于管理者是否尊重它，也取决于员工是否敢放心使用它。这个争论之所以重要，是因为它在问一个现代生活中的基本问题：技术究竟应该扩大自由，还是只是延长工作时间？
