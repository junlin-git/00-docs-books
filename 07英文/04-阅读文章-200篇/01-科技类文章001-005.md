## 文章 01：The Rise of Artificial Intelligence

**难度**：B2 | **字数**：约380词 | **主题**：人工智能发展

---

Artificial intelligence, or AI, has moved from the realm of science fiction into our everyday lives faster than most people anticipated. From the recommendation algorithms that suggest what we watch on streaming platforms to the voice assistants that answer our questions, AI is quietly reshaping how we live and work.

At its core, AI refers to computer systems capable of performing tasks that typically require human intelligence — tasks such as recognising speech, translating languages, making decisions, and even creating artwork. The technology behind modern AI is largely based on machine learning, a technique where systems learn from vast amounts of data rather than following pre-programmed rules.

One of the most dramatic recent developments has been the emergence of large language models (LLMs) — AI systems trained on enormous text datasets that can generate remarkably human-like text. These models can write essays, summarise documents, answer complex questions, and even write code. Their potential applications span education, healthcare, law, and creative industries.

However, the rapid advancement of AI has also raised significant concerns. Critics worry about job displacement, as automation increasingly threatens roles previously considered safe from technological substitution. There are also debates around algorithmic bias — the tendency of AI systems to reflect or even amplify the prejudices present in their training data.

Privacy is another major issue. AI systems often require large quantities of personal data to function effectively, raising questions about who controls this data and how it might be misused. Governments around the world are scrambling to establish regulatory frameworks that can keep pace with this fast-moving technology.

Despite these challenges, the potential benefits of AI are enormous. In medicine, AI can analyse medical images with greater accuracy than human radiologists, potentially saving millions of lives by detecting diseases earlier. In climate science, AI is helping researchers model complex environmental systems and develop new clean energy solutions.

The key challenge for society is not whether AI will continue to advance — that much seems certain — but how we can harness its power responsibly, ensuring that its benefits are widely shared and its risks carefully managed.

---

### 全文翻译

人工智能，简称 AI，已经比大多数人预料得更快地从科幻小说的领域进入了我们的日常生活。从在流媒体平台上推荐我们观看内容的推荐算法，到回答我们问题的语音助手，AI 正在悄然重塑我们的生活和工作方式。

从本质上讲，AI 指的是能够执行通常需要人类智能的任务的计算机系统，例如识别语音、翻译语言、做出决策，甚至创作艺术作品。现代 AI 背后的技术主要建立在机器学习之上，这是一种让系统从海量数据中学习、而不是遵循预先编写规则的技术。

近年来最引人注目的发展之一，是大语言模型（LLM）的出现。这类 AI 系统在规模巨大的文本数据集上训练，能够生成极其像人类撰写的文本。这些模型可以写文章、总结文档、回答复杂问题，甚至编写代码。它们的潜在应用遍及教育、医疗、法律和创意产业等领域。

然而，AI 的迅速进步也引发了广泛担忧。批评者担心就业岗位被取代，因为自动化正越来越多地威胁那些过去被认为不易被技术替代的工作。同时，人们也在争论算法偏见问题，也就是 AI 系统会反映甚至放大训练数据中已有的偏见。

隐私也是另一个重大问题。AI 系统通常需要大量个人数据才能有效运行，这引发了关于谁控制这些数据以及这些数据如何被滥用的问题。世界各国政府都在争相建立能够跟上这种快速发展技术的监管框架。

尽管存在这些挑战，AI 的潜在益处仍然极其巨大。在医学领域，AI 可以比人类放射科医生更准确地分析医学影像，从而通过更早发现疾病而可能挽救数百万人的生命。在气候科学中，AI 正帮助研究人员为复杂的环境系统建模，并开发新的清洁能源解决方案。

对社会而言，关键挑战并不在于 AI 是否会继续进步，这一点似乎已成定局，而在于我们如何负责任地利用它的力量，确保它的收益能够被广泛共享，同时其风险得到谨慎管理。

## 文章 02：How Smartphones Changed Human Behavior

**难度**：B2 | **字数**：约350词 | **主题**：智能手机与人类行为

---

Less than two decades ago, the idea of carrying a pocket-sized computer that could access the sum of human knowledge, take professional-quality photographs, and connect you instantly with anyone on the planet would have seemed extraordinary. Today, for billions of people, it is simply called a smartphone — and life without one is almost unimaginable.

The smartphone has not merely changed how we communicate; it has fundamentally altered our relationship with time, attention, and memory. Research suggests that the average person now checks their phone over 150 times a day. We reach for our devices in moments of boredom, anxiety, and even mid-conversation, a habit that psychologists have linked to what they call "nomophobia" — the fear of being without a mobile phone.

One of the most profound changes has been to our capacity for sustained attention. A widely cited study suggested that since the widespread adoption of smartphones, average human attention spans have shortened. While this specific statistic has been disputed, there is broad consensus among researchers that the constant availability of instant gratification — whether from social media likes, news feeds, or entertainment — has trained our brains to seek novelty over depth.

Memory has also been affected. Because we know we can look up almost any fact instantly, we are less motivated to commit information to long-term memory. Cognitive scientists call this "cognitive offloading" — the tendency to outsource mental tasks to external devices.

Yet the smartphone has also brought extraordinary benefits. For people in remote areas, it provides access to banking, healthcare information, and educational resources that were previously unavailable. For the visually impaired, accessibility features have opened up digital communication. During the COVID-19 pandemic, smartphones became lifelines for millions of isolated individuals.

The challenge is not to demonise the technology, but to develop a more conscious relationship with it — one where we dictate to our devices, rather than letting our devices dictate to us.

---

### 全文翻译

不到二十年前，如果有人设想口袋里装着一台袖珍电脑，它既能获取人类几乎全部知识、拍摄专业水准的照片，又能让你立刻与地球上任何人联系，这听起来都会十分不可思议。如今，对数十亿人来说，这种设备不过就叫作智能手机，而没有它的生活几乎难以想象。

智能手机不仅改变了我们的沟通方式；它还从根本上改变了我们与时间、注意力和记忆的关系。研究表明，普通人现在每天查看手机超过 150 次。我们会在无聊、焦虑，甚至正在谈话时伸手去拿设备，这种习惯被心理学家与所谓的“无手机焦虑症”联系起来，也就是害怕身边没有手机。

最深刻的变化之一，发生在我们持续专注的能力上。一项被广泛引用的研究认为，自从智能手机普及以来，人类平均注意力持续时间已经缩短。尽管这一具体统计数据曾受到质疑，研究人员之间仍普遍认同：即时满足的持续可得性，无论来自社交媒体点赞、新闻信息流，还是娱乐内容，都在训练我们的大脑优先追求新鲜感，而不是深度。

记忆也受到了影响。因为我们知道几乎任何事实都能立刻查到，所以我们把信息存入长期记忆的动力减弱了。认知科学家把这种现象称为“认知卸载”，也就是把原本由大脑完成的任务交给外部设备。

然而，智能手机也带来了非凡的好处。对于偏远地区的人来说，它让他们能够获得此前无法得到的银行服务、医疗信息和教育资源。对于视障人士而言，无障碍功能打开了数字沟通的大门。在新冠疫情期间，智能手机更成为数百万与世隔绝者的生命线。

真正的挑战不在于妖魔化这项技术，而在于与它建立一种更有意识的关系，让我们来支配设备，而不是让设备来支配我们。

## 文章 03：The Promise and Peril of Genetic Engineering

**难度**：C1 | **字数**：约400词 | **主题**：基因工程

---

The ability to edit the genetic code of living organisms has long been the stuff of science fiction. With the development of CRISPR-Cas9 technology in the early 2010s, that ability became reality — and with it came both extraordinary promise and profound ethical questions.

CRISPR (Clustered Regularly Interspaced Short Palindromic Repeats) is a molecular tool that allows scientists to cut, modify, or replace specific sections of DNA with unprecedented precision and at a fraction of the cost of previous methods. Unlike earlier gene-editing tools, CRISPR is fast, affordable, and accessible to laboratories worldwide.

The medical applications are breathtaking. Scientists are already using CRISPR to develop treatments for sickle cell disease, a painful and life-threatening hereditary condition. Clinical trials are underway for CRISPR-based therapies targeting certain cancers, HIV, and inherited blindness. In the future, the technology might eliminate genetic diseases from human populations entirely.

Beyond medicine, CRISPR holds promise for agriculture. Researchers have developed disease-resistant crops, drought-tolerant wheat, and even cattle that produce less methane — potentially helping both food security and the climate. The technology could also be used to revive extinct species or protect endangered ones.

However, the same capabilities that make CRISPR so powerful also make it dangerous. In 2018, a Chinese scientist shocked the world by announcing the birth of twin girls whose embryos had been gene-edited — the first known case of heritable human genome editing. The scientific community reacted with near-universal condemnation. The experiment raised the spectre of "designer babies" — the selection and enhancement of traits such as intelligence, physical appearance, and athletic ability based on genetic manipulation.

The ethical concerns extend beyond individual cases. Who controls access to genetic modification? Will it be available only to the wealthy, creating a new form of biological inequality? How do we ensure that modifications do not spread unpredictably through ecosystems?

These are questions without easy answers, but they demand urgent attention. The history of technology suggests that once a capability exists, restraint is difficult. The challenge is to build robust international governance frameworks before the technology races beyond our ability to regulate it.

---

### 全文翻译

长期以来，编辑生物体遗传密码的能力一直像是科幻小说中的情节。随着 2010 年代初 CRISPR-Cas9 技术的发展，这种能力变成了现实，而随之而来的既有非凡的希望，也有深刻的伦理问题。

CRISPR（规律成簇间隔短回文重复序列）是一种分子工具，它使科学家能够以前所未有的精确度，并且以远低于以往方法的成本，切割、修改或替换 DNA 的特定片段。与早期基因编辑工具不同，CRISPR 速度更快、成本更低，也更容易被世界各地的实验室使用。

它在医学上的应用令人惊叹。科学家已经在利用 CRISPR 开发治疗镰状细胞病的方法，这是一种痛苦且危及生命的遗传性疾病。针对某些癌症、艾滋病和遗传性失明的 CRISPR 疗法临床试验也已展开。未来，这项技术甚至可能把遗传疾病从人类群体中彻底消除。

除了医学之外，CRISPR 在农业领域也充满希望。研究人员已经培育出抗病作物、耐旱小麦，甚至能产生更少甲烷的牛，这对粮食安全和气候问题都可能有所帮助。这项技术还可能被用来复活灭绝物种，或保护濒危物种。

然而，正是让 CRISPR 如此强大的那些能力，也使它具有危险性。2018 年，一位中国科学家宣布一对经过基因编辑胚胎出生的双胞胎女婴诞生，震惊了世界。这是已知首例可遗传人类基因组编辑案例。科学界几乎一致表示谴责。这项实验让“定制婴儿”的幽灵浮现出来，也就是通过基因操控来选择和增强智力、外貌、运动能力等特征。

伦理担忧并不限于个别案例。谁来控制获取基因改造的权利？这项技术会不会只向富人开放，从而造成一种新的生物不平等？我们又该如何确保这些改动不会在生态系统中不可预测地扩散？

这些问题并没有简单答案，但它们迫切需要被认真对待。技术史表明，一旦某种能力存在，克制就会变得困难。真正的挑战，是在这项技术发展到超出我们监管能力之前，建立起稳健的国际治理框架。

## 文章 04：The Internet of Things: A Connected Future

**难度**：B2 | **字数**：约340词 | **主题**：物联网

---

Imagine waking up to an alarm that has already adjusted based on your sleep patterns, a coffee machine that begins brewing as soon as it detects you're awake, and a refrigerator that automatically orders groceries when supplies run low. This vision of a seamlessly connected home is no longer science fiction — it is the promise of the Internet of Things (IoT).

The Internet of Things refers to the network of physical devices — from smartphones and smart speakers to industrial sensors and medical implants — that are embedded with software, sensors, and connectivity, enabling them to collect and exchange data. Analysts estimate that by 2030, there could be over 50 billion connected devices worldwide.

In homes, IoT enables smarter energy management. Smart thermostats learn residents' schedules and preferences, reducing heating and cooling costs by up to 20%. Smart meters provide real-time energy usage data, helping households cut waste. In cities, IoT sensors monitor traffic flow, street lighting, and air quality, enabling more efficient and sustainable urban management.

In industry, the implications are even more significant. Sensors on factory equipment can detect early signs of mechanical failure, allowing preventive maintenance before costly breakdowns occur. In agriculture, soil sensors and weather monitoring systems help farmers optimise irrigation and reduce water waste. In healthcare, wearable devices continuously monitor patients' vital signs, alerting medical staff to changes that might indicate a developing condition.

Yet the proliferation of connected devices also creates significant security and privacy challenges. Each device represents a potential entry point for hackers. In 2016, a massive cyberattack exploited hundreds of thousands of poorly secured IoT devices to take down major websites, demonstrating the vulnerability of our increasingly connected infrastructure.

As IoT devices collect vast amounts of data about our lives, questions about data ownership, surveillance, and corporate responsibility become increasingly pressing. The challenge is to realise the enormous potential of IoT technology while establishing the security standards and regulatory protections necessary to prevent it from becoming a tool of intrusion rather than convenience.

---

### 全文翻译

想象一下，你醒来时，闹钟已经根据你的睡眠模式自动调整；咖啡机一检测到你醒来就开始煮咖啡；冰箱在食材快用完时会自动下单购买杂货。这个无缝连接家庭的愿景已经不再是科幻，它正是物联网（IoT）所承诺的未来。

物联网指的是由各种实体设备组成的网络，从智能手机和智能音箱，到工业传感器和医疗植入物，这些设备都嵌入了软件、传感器和连接能力，因此能够收集并交换数据。分析人士估计，到 2030 年，全球联网设备数量可能超过 500 亿台。

在家庭中，物联网可以实现更智能的能源管理。智能恒温器会学习住户的日程和偏好，将供暖和制冷成本最多降低 20%。智能电表提供实时用能数据，帮助家庭减少浪费。在城市里，物联网传感器会监测交通流量、路灯和空气质量，从而实现更高效、更可持续的城市管理。

在工业领域，其影响更为显著。安装在工厂设备上的传感器可以发现机械故障的早期迹象，使企业能在代价高昂的停机发生之前进行预防性维护。在农业中，土壤传感器和气象监测系统帮助农民优化灌溉并减少水资源浪费。在医疗领域，可穿戴设备持续监测病人的生命体征，并在变化可能表明病情发展时提醒医护人员。

然而，联网设备的激增也带来了重大的安全和隐私挑战。每一台设备都可能成为黑客入侵的入口。2016 年，一场大规模网络攻击利用了数十万台安全防护薄弱的物联网设备，导致多个大型网站瘫痪，这表明我们日益互联的基础设施其实相当脆弱。

随着物联网设备收集了大量关于我们生活的数据，围绕数据所有权、监控以及企业责任的问题变得越来越紧迫。关键挑战在于，在建立必要安全标准和监管保护措施的同时，实现物联网技术的巨大潜力，避免它从便利工具变成侵入工具。

## 文章 05：Space Tourism: A New Frontier

**难度**：B2 | **字数**：约350词 | **主题**：太空旅游

---

For most of human history, space was the exclusive domain of trained astronauts working for national governments. In the past decade, however, a new industry has emerged that promises to open the cosmos to paying customers — space tourism.

Companies like SpaceX, Blue Origin, and Virgin Galactic have invested billions of dollars in developing commercial spacecraft capable of carrying civilian passengers. In 2021, Bezos' Blue Origin completed its first crewed flight to the edge of space, while SpaceX's Inspiration4 mission sent four civilians on a three-day orbital journey — the first all-civilian spaceflight in history.

Supporters argue that space tourism will drive technological innovation, reduce launch costs through competition, and ultimately advance humanity's expansion into the solar system. Just as commercial aviation transformed global travel in the 20th century, commercial spaceflight could do the same for access to space.

Critics, however, raise important concerns. Rocket launches produce significant carbon emissions and other pollutants, contributing to upper atmosphere damage at a time when climate change is already a critical concern. The current clientele of space tourists are exclusively the ultra-wealthy — tickets for a suborbital flight can cost hundreds of thousands of dollars. Is it ethical, critics ask, to burn enormous amounts of fossil fuels so that billionaires can enjoy a brief view of the Earth from space?

There are also safety concerns. Space remains an extraordinarily hostile environment. The history of space exploration is marked by tragic accidents, and expanding access to untrained civilians inevitably increases risk.

Yet proponents counter that every transformative technology begins as a luxury of the elite before costs fall and access broadens. Early commercial aviation was similarly expensive and available only to the wealthy. The question is not whether space tourism is worth pursuing, but how to do so responsibly — with proper safety regulations, environmental mitigation, and a vision for how this technology can ultimately benefit all of humanity.

---

### 全文翻译

在人类历史的大部分时间里，太空一直是受过专业训练、为国家政府工作的宇航员的专属领域。然而在过去十年里，一个新兴产业出现了，它承诺将宇宙向付费乘客开放，这就是太空旅游。

像 SpaceX、Blue Origin 和 Virgin Galactic 这样的公司，已经投入数十亿美元开发能够搭载普通乘客的商业航天器。2021 年，贝索斯的 Blue Origin 完成了首次载人飞行，抵达太空边缘；与此同时，SpaceX 的 Inspiration4 任务让四名平民完成了一次为期三天的轨道旅行，这是历史上第一次完全由平民组成的载人航天飞行。

支持者认为，太空旅游将推动技术创新，通过竞争降低发射成本，并最终推进人类向太阳系扩张。正如商业航空在 20 世纪改变了全球旅行方式一样，商业航天也可能对太空准入产生同样的作用。

然而，批评者提出了重要担忧。火箭发射会产生大量碳排放和其他污染物，在气候变化已经十分严峻的当下，这会对高层大气造成伤害。当前太空旅游的顾客几乎完全是超级富豪，一张亚轨道飞行票价就可能高达数十万美元。批评者问：为了让亿万富翁短暂地从太空俯瞰地球，就燃烧巨量化石燃料，这在伦理上合理吗？

人们也担心安全问题。太空依然是一个极其恶劣的环境。太空探索的历史充满悲剧事故，而把这类活动开放给未经训练的普通人，必然会增加风险。

但支持者反驳说，任何具有变革性的技术，在成本下降、普及扩展之前，最初都只是精英的奢侈品。早期商业航空同样昂贵，也只服务富人。问题不在于太空旅游值不值得追求，而在于如何以负责任的方式推进它，包括完善的安全监管、环境缓解措施，以及一个能让这项技术最终造福全人类的愿景。
