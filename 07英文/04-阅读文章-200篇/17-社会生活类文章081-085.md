## 文章 81：The Return of Community Gardens

**难度**：B1-B2 | **字数**：约250词 | **主题**：社区花园

---

In many cities, empty lots once filled with rubbish or weeds are being turned into community gardens. Residents share small pieces of land to grow tomatoes, herbs, beans, and flowers. For some people, the attraction is practical: fresh food is expensive, and gardening offers a cheaper way to bring vegetables home. For others, the bigger value is social. A garden gives neighbors a reason to meet, exchange advice, and spend time outdoors together.

Community gardens also change the feeling of a neighborhood. A place that looked abandoned can become cleaner, safer, and more welcoming when local people care for it. Children learn where food comes from. Older residents can pass on skills that are not often taught in classrooms. In areas where many people live in apartments without private outdoor space, a shared garden becomes a rare common ground.

These projects are not always easy to manage. People may disagree about tools, water use, or how the harvest should be shared. Some gardens disappear when the land is sold for new construction. Even so, their popularity says something important about urban life. In an age of screens and fast services, many people still want slow, physical activities that connect them to both nature and one another. A small garden cannot solve every city problem, but it can remind people that local community is something they can build with their own hands.

### 全文翻译

在许多城市，曾经堆满垃圾或杂草的空地，正被改造成社区花园。居民们共同使用小块土地种植番茄、香草、豆类和花卉。对有些人来说，它的吸引力是很实际的：新鲜食物价格不低，而种菜能以更低成本把蔬菜带回家。对另一些人来说，更大的价值在于社交。花园给邻居们提供了见面、交换经验并一起在户外度过时间的理由。

社区花园也会改变一个社区的整体感觉。当本地居民共同照料一个地方时，原本看起来被遗弃的空间会变得更整洁、更安全，也更让人愿意接近。孩子们可以学习食物从哪里来。年长居民则能把课堂里不常教授的技能传递给下一代。在许多人住在没有私人户外空间公寓的地区，这种共享花园就成了少见的公共场所。

这些项目并不总是容易管理。人们可能会对工具使用、浇水方式或收成如何分配产生分歧。有些花园会在土地被出售用于新开发后消失。即便如此，它们的流行仍说明了城市生活中的一个重要事实。在屏幕和快速服务主导的时代，许多人依然渴望缓慢、真实、能把自己同时连接到自然和他人的活动。一个小花园不能解决所有城市问题，但它能提醒人们，本地社区是可以用双手一点点建起来的。

## 文章 82：Why More People Are Choosing to Rent Clothes

**难度**：B1-B2 | **字数**：约245词 | **主题**：服装租赁

---

Fashion has long been connected with ownership. People bought clothes, kept them for years, or threw them away when trends changed. Recently, however, clothing rental services have grown popular, especially among young professionals in large cities. Instead of buying a new dress, suit, or designer coat for a single event, customers borrow it for a few days and return it. This model fits a generation that is used to streaming music, sharing cars, and paying for access rather than possession.

There are several reasons for the shift. Renting clothes can be cheaper than purchasing high-quality items, especially for weddings, job interviews, or formal parties. It also allows people to experiment with style without filling their homes with rarely used pieces. Environmental concerns matter as well. The fashion industry creates large amounts of waste, and many consumers are uncomfortable buying something they may wear only once. Rental platforms promise a way to reduce overconsumption, though the transport and cleaning involved also have an environmental cost.

The trend reveals a broader change in values. For many people, flexibility now matters more than ownership. At the same time, rental culture raises questions about identity and status. Does borrowing a luxury item make fashion more democratic, or does it simply keep people attached to the same status symbols? The answer may depend on how people use these services. Still, the growth of clothing rental shows that social habits can change quickly when convenience, economy, and new ideas about sustainability meet.

### 全文翻译

长期以来，时尚都与“拥有”联系在一起。人们买下衣服，穿上几年，或者在潮流变化后把它们丢掉。不过最近，服装租赁服务越来越受欢迎，尤其是在大城市的年轻职场人中。顾客不再为一次活动购买新的礼服、西装或设计师外套，而是先借用几天，再归还。这种模式很适合已经习惯于音乐流媒体、共享汽车，以及“为使用权付费而不是为占有权付费”的一代人。

这种变化有几个原因。租衣服有时比直接购买高质量服装更便宜，尤其适用于婚礼、求职面试或正式聚会。它也让人们可以尝试不同风格，而不必把很少穿的衣物塞满家里。环保顾虑同样重要。时尚产业制造了大量浪费，许多消费者也不愿意为只穿一次的东西付钱。租赁平台声称能减少过度消费，虽然运输和清洁本身也会带来环境成本。

这一趋势反映了价值观的更广泛变化。对许多人来说，灵活性如今比拥有更重要。同时，租赁文化也提出了关于身份和地位的问题。借穿奢侈品，究竟是在让时尚更民主，还是只是在用另一种方式让人继续依附同样的身份符号？答案可能取决于人们如何使用这些服务。但无论如何，服装租赁的增长都说明，当便利、经济压力和新的可持续观念相遇时，社会习惯会很快改变。

## 文章 83：The Quiet Growth of Solo Dining

**难度**：B1-B2 | **字数**：约240词 | **主题**：独自用餐

---

Eating alone in a restaurant once carried a social stigma in many places. A person sitting by themselves was often seen as lonely, waiting for someone, or lacking friends. That perception is changing. In large cities, solo dining has become common among students, office workers, travelers, and even retirees. Restaurants in Japan, South Korea, Europe, and North America have responded by designing spaces that make single customers feel comfortable rather than awkward.

Several forces are driving this shift. Urban life is more individualised than before, and many adults live alone or keep irregular schedules. Remote work and flexible hours also mean people no longer eat lunch at the same time as colleagues. In addition, social attitudes have changed. Many people now see eating alone as a form of freedom. It allows them to choose their own restaurant, pace, and conversation level. Some bring a book; others simply enjoy a short break from constant social contact.

Solo dining also tells us something about modern independence. Being comfortable alone is increasingly treated as a useful life skill rather than a sad condition. Of course, restaurants are still social spaces, and many people continue to value shared meals deeply. But the rise of solo dining suggests that everyday public life is becoming more accepting of different ways to use common spaces. In that sense, one person at a table is no longer necessarily a sign of isolation. It may be a sign of confidence, convenience, or simply a different rhythm of life.

### 全文翻译

过去，在很多地方，独自一人在餐馆吃饭常常带有社会污名。一个人单独坐着，常会被看成孤独、在等人，或者没有朋友。这种看法正在改变。在大城市里，独自用餐已经成为学生、上班族、旅行者甚至退休人士的常见选择。日本、韩国、欧洲和北美的餐馆也做出了回应，开始设计更适合单人顾客的空间，让他们感到舒服而不是尴尬。

这种变化由多种力量推动。城市生活比过去更个人化，许多成年人独居，或有着不规律的作息。远程办公和弹性工作时间也意味着，人们不再总是和同事在同一时间吃午饭。此外，社会态度也在变化。很多人如今把独自吃饭看作一种自由。它允许自己决定去哪里吃、吃多快，以及要不要交谈。有些人会带一本书，有些人则只是享受从持续社交接触中短暂抽离的片刻。

独自用餐也说明了现代独立性的某种变化。能够自在地独处，越来越被视为一种有用的生活能力，而不是令人难过的状态。当然，餐馆仍然是社交空间，很多人依旧深深看重共享一餐的意义。但独食现象的增长表明，日常公共生活正在更包容地接受不同的人使用共同空间的方式。从这个意义上说，一个人坐在桌边不再一定意味着隔离，它也可能意味着自信、便利，或者只是另一种生活节奏。

## 文章 84：How Neighborhood Apps Change Local Life

**难度**：B2 | **字数**：约245词 | **主题**：社区应用

---

Before smartphones, local information often traveled slowly. People learned about a lost pet, a power cut, or a suspicious noise by speaking to neighbors or reading notices on a building door. Today, neighborhood apps and online groups allow residents to exchange this information instantly. A broken streetlight can be reported within minutes. Parents can recommend tutors. Someone moving away can give away old furniture to people nearby.

These platforms can strengthen local ties, especially in large cities where many residents barely know the people living next door. A digital message board lowers the social cost of asking small questions or offering help. During storms, blackouts, or public health emergencies, such tools can become especially valuable. They help people share updates quickly and organize support for elderly or vulnerable residents.

Yet neighborhood apps can also create new tensions. Minor complaints may spread faster than face-to-face conversations would allow. Rumors about strangers can turn into unfair suspicion. Some groups become dominated by a few loud voices, while quieter residents stop participating. There is also a question of privacy: not everyone wants local life to be recorded, mapped, and discussed online. Neighborhood apps are therefore neither purely good nor purely harmful. Like any social tool, they reflect the habits of the people using them. When used responsibly, they can make local communities more responsive and practical. When used poorly, they can turn ordinary living into unnecessary conflict.

### 全文翻译

在智能手机出现之前，本地信息的传播常常很慢。人们要么通过和邻居交谈，要么通过门口张贴的通知，才知道宠物走失、停电或附近有可疑声音。如今，社区应用和线上群组让居民可以几乎即时地交换这些信息。一盏坏掉的路灯几分钟内就能被报告。家长们可以互相推荐家教。有人搬家时，也能把旧家具送给住在附近的人。

这些平台可以增强本地联系，尤其是在大城市里，很多居民几乎不认识隔壁住的人。数字留言板降低了人们提小问题或提供帮助的社交成本。在暴风雨、停电或公共卫生紧急事件期间，这类工具尤其有价值。它们能帮助人们快速分享更新，并为老年人或脆弱群体组织支援。

然而，社区应用也会制造新的紧张关系。原本很小的抱怨，比面对面交流更容易快速扩散。关于陌生人的传言可能演变成不公平的怀疑。有些群组被少数声音很大的人主导，较安静的居民则慢慢退出。隐私也是问题之一：并不是每个人都希望自己的社区生活被在线记录、标注和讨论。因此，社区应用既不是纯粹有益的，也不是纯粹有害的。和所有社会工具一样，它们反映的是使用者本身的习惯。使用得当时，它们能让地方社区更灵活、更实用；使用不当时，它们也可能把普通生活变成不必要的冲突。

## 文章 85：The New Etiquette of Voice Messages

**难度**：B1-B2 | **字数**：约235词 | **主题**：语音消息礼仪

---

Texting changed personal communication by making messages fast, silent, and easy to answer later. Now voice messages are changing it again. On many social platforms, people increasingly send short audio recordings instead of typing. A voice note can feel warmer and more personal than text because it includes tone, emotion, and rhythm. For busy people walking to work or cooking dinner, speaking can also be easier than writing.

However, convenience for the sender does not always mean convenience for the receiver. A text can be read quickly in almost any place, but a voice message requires headphones, privacy, or at least a quiet moment. Some people love voice notes because they sound intimate and natural. Others find them inefficient, especially when the message could have been one short sentence. This difference has created a new area of social etiquette that many people are still negotiating.

Questions now arise that previous generations never had to ask. How long is too long for a voice message? Is it rude to reply with text when someone sends audio? Should work-related voice notes be avoided outside office hours? The rise of voice messaging shows how technology creates not only new tools but also new manners. In every era, communication requires shared expectations. Voice messages are a small example of a larger truth: when technology changes how we speak, society has to invent fresh rules for politeness, efficiency, and respect.

### 全文翻译

短信让私人沟通变得更快、更安静，也更方便稍后回复。如今，语音消息又在改变这一切。在许多社交平台上，人们越来越常用简短音频而不是打字。语音消息比文字更有温度、更个人化，因为它包含语气、情绪和节奏。对那些一边走路上班、一边做饭的忙碌人来说，开口说话有时也比写字更容易。

然而，发送者觉得方便，并不一定意味着接收者也会觉得方便。文字几乎可以在任何地方迅速阅读，但语音消息往往需要耳机、隐私，或者至少需要一个相对安静的时刻。有些人喜欢语音留言，因为它听起来更亲近、更自然。另一些人则觉得它效率低，尤其是当内容原本只需一句短句就能说清时。这种差异创造了一个很多人仍在摸索的新礼仪领域。

于是，一些前几代人根本不会遇到的问题出现了。语音消息多长才算太长？别人发语音时，用文字回复是否失礼？工作相关的语音消息是否应该避免在下班后发送？语音留言的兴起说明，技术带来的不只是新工具，还有新的相处方式。任何时代的沟通都需要共享预期。语音消息只是一个小例子，它反映出更大的事实：当技术改变了我们说话的方式，社会也必须重新发明关于礼貌、效率和尊重的规则。
