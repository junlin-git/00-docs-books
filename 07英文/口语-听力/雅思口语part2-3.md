# 雅思口语 Part 2 高质量范例 30 篇

> 使用原则：不要逐字背整篇。每篇背框架、亮点搭配、核心故事，然后复述、改写、套题。

---

## 01 影响你的长辈

**题目：Describe a family member who influenced you.**

**范例：**
I’d like to talk about my grandmother. She’s in her seventies, but she’s still incredibly active. She ran a small grocery store for over thirty years and raised three children at the same time. What impresses me most is her attitude when things go wrong. She never panics; she just says, “Let’s fix one thing at a time.” That sentence has stayed with me. Whenever I feel overwhelmed by exams or work, I remind myself of her calmness. She didn’t give me long lectures; she showed me how to be patient and resilient through her daily actions. That’s why she’s had a huge influence on me.

**好的地方：**
- 有具体身份、年龄、职业。
- 直接引用一句话，真实感强。
- 从“她”转到“我”，体现影响。

**背诵理解的地方：**
- 框架：who → 背景 → 品质 → 例子 → 对我的影响。
- 亮点：incredibly active, raise three children, fix one thing at a time, overwhelmed, resilient。
- 理解：影响不是靠说教，而是靠行动和态度。

**可套题：** 敬佩的人、给你建议的人、家人、老人。

## 02 有技能的朋友

**题目：Describe a friend who has a useful skill.**

**范例：**
My friend Leo is amazing at photography. He taught himself by watching videos and practicing almost every day. He can turn an ordinary street into something beautiful. Last month, he helped me take photos for my blog. Instead of just clicking the shutter, he explained how light, angle, and patience matter. What I admire is not only his skill but also his willingness to share it. He never makes me feel stupid. He often says, “Look at the light, not the object.” That simple idea changed how I see everyday life.

**好的地方：**
- 技能具体：摄影。
- 有事件：帮 blog 拍照。
- 有金句：Look at the light, not the object.

**背诵理解的地方：**
- 亮点：taught himself, turn A into B, click the shutter, willingness to share。
- 理解：夸朋友不能只说“他很好”，要讲他具体怎么帮你。

**可套题：** 聪明的人、有才华的人、你想学的人、乐于助人的人。

## 03 有趣的邻居

**题目：Describe an interesting neighbor.**

**范例：**
I’d like to talk about Mr. Chen, a retired engineer who lives next door. His apartment is full of old clocks, and you can hear them ticking all day. He repairs them as a hobby, even though he doesn’t make much money from it. What makes him interesting is that he knows almost everyone on our street. He organizes clean-up days and helps elderly neighbors with small repairs. He has the patience of an old craftsman and the curiosity of a child. Talking with him always makes me slow down and notice small details in life.

**好的地方：**
- 画面感强：满屋钟表、滴答声。
- 性格鲜明：老工匠的耐心 + 孩子的好奇。
- 社区感强。

**背诵理解的地方：**
- 亮点：retired engineer, ticking, as a hobby, clean-up days, old craftsman。
- 理解：有趣不等于搞笑，也可以是特别的生活方式。

**可套题：** 老人、社区、乐于助人的人、你敬佩的人。

## 04 喜欢的老师

**题目：Describe a teacher you like.**

**范例：**
My favorite teacher is Ms. Lin, my high school English teacher. She didn’t just teach grammar; she made us debate, act out dialogues, and share opinions. Whenever we made mistakes, she corrected us gently and said, “Mistakes are proof that you’re trying.” That made me feel safe enough to speak. She also used movies and songs to make class lively. Because of her, I stopped being afraid of English and started to enjoy expressing myself. Even now, when I lack confidence, I remember her words.

**好的地方：**
- 教学方法具体：debate, act out, movies。
- 金句自然。
- 影响清楚：从害怕到享受。

**背诵理解的地方：**
- 亮点：correct gently, mistakes are proof, feel safe enough to speak。
- 理解：好老师题重点不是“她很好”，而是“她怎么改变你”。

**可套题：** 榜样、学校、学习经历、影响你的人。

## 05 放松的地方

**题目：Describe a place you like to relax.**

**范例：**
I’d like to talk about a riverside park near my home. It’s not fancy, but it has a long walking path, old trees, and a quiet bench facing the water. I usually go there on weekend mornings. I leave my phone in my bag, watch people jogging, and listen to the birds. Sometimes I just sit and do nothing. The reason it helps me relax is that it feels like an escape from my daily routine. I don’t think about deadlines or messages. After an hour there, I feel recharged and ready for the week.

**好的地方：**
- 地点细节：walking path, bench, water。
- 行为具体：手机放包里、看人慢跑。
- 结果清楚：recharged。

**背诵理解的地方：**
- 亮点：not fancy, facing the water, escape from routine, recharged。
- 理解：放松题要讲“不做什么”，比只讲“做什么”更高级。

**可套题：** 安静的地方、常去的地方、推荐的地方、开心的地方。

## 09 一次成功

**题目：Describe a success you had.**

**范例：**
A success I’m proud of is finishing my first 10-kilometer run. At the beginning, I could only run for two kilometers before I felt out of breath. So I trained three times a week for about three months. On race day, it rained heavily, and I almost gave up halfway. But I told myself to just keep moving. When I crossed the finish line, I wasn’t fast, but I felt amazing. It taught me that success isn’t always about winning; sometimes it’s about proving to yourself that you can keep going.

**好的地方：**
- 有过程：从2公里到10公里。
- 有困难：下雨、想放弃。
- 有感悟：成功是证明自己能坚持。

**背诵理解的地方：**
- 亮点：out of breath, trained three times a week, almost gave up, keep moving, cross the finish line。
- 理解：成功题要有“困难—坚持—结果—意义”。

**可套题：** 挑战、目标、健康、难忘经历。

## 10 一次失败

The examiner looked at me and said, “Maybe you need more practice.” I felt terrible at that moment. But later, I realized that failure was actually useful feedback. I practiced reversing for hours and watched videos online. The second time, I passed easily. Now I see that failure isn’t the opposite of success; it’s part of the process. It teaches you exactly what you need to improve.

**好的地方：**
- 失败具体：倒车撞杆。
- 有转折：失败 → 反馈 → 通过。
- 金句：failure isn’t opposite of success。

**背诵理解的地方：**
- 亮点：embarrassing, hit a pole, useful feedback, part of the process。
- 理解：失败题不要只卖惨，要讲你学到什么。

**可套题：** 教训、决定、改变、挑战。

## 01 影响你的长辈

**Q1: Why do older people often have a strong influence on young people?**
Well, I think it’s mainly because older people have more life experience. They’ve been through setbacks, so their advice tends to be practical rather than theoretical. Having said that, young people don’t always follow older people’s advice, especially in a fast-changing world. So I’d say the influence is strong, but it’s more about character than direct instructions.

**Q2: Do young people today respect older people as much as before?**
To be honest, I think it’s mixed. On the one hand, young people still respect elders in many families, especially during festivals or important decisions. On the other hand, the internet has made young people more independent, so they question traditional authority more. They may respect experience but not blindly obey. **好的地方：** 有让步，不极端；用 practical rather than theoretical、passed down、blindly obey 提升表达。

Children learn more from behavior than lectures.

**好的地方：** judgment, perfectionism, unlearned, model resilience。

## 21 理想工作

**Q1: What makes a job satisfying?**
A satisfying job usually has meaning, autonomy, and good colleagues. Fair pay matters, of course, but money alone doesn’t keep people happy. People want to feel respected and see progress. For example, a travel writer may earn less but enjoy freedom and creativity. Having said that, unstable income can be stressful. So the ideal job balances passion with practical security.

**Q2: How will jobs change in the future?**
Jobs will change a lot because of automation and AI. Some routine jobs will disappear, while new jobs will appear in technology, care, and creative fields. Remote work and gig work may become more common. That means workers need to keep learning and adapt. Governments and companies should also provide retraining. So the future of work is flexible, but it requires preparation.

**好的地方：** autonomy, practical security, automation, retraining。  
**背诵理解：** 工作题 = 满足感 + 意义/自由 + 未来变化 + 再培训。

## 22 远程办公/网课

**Q1: Is remote work good for everyone?**
Not necessarily. Remote work is great for people who need flexibility, hate commuting, or want to focus. But it can be lonely and blur the line between work and rest. Some people also lack a quiet space at home. For example, I studied online during the pandemic and liked saving time, but I missed classmates. So I think hybrid work is often the best compromise. It offers freedom and human contact.

**Q2: How can online learning be improved?**
Online learning can be improved by making it more interactive. Long videos are boring, so shorter modules, quizzes, and discussions help. Teachers should give feedback and create small group activities. Students also need a routine and a dedicated space. For example, studying in 25-minute blocks helped me focus. So online learning works best when it is structured, social, and flexible.

**好的地方：** blur the line, hybrid, interactive, dedicated space。  
**背诵理解：** 远程题 = 优缺点 + 混合办公 + 互动 + 结构/社交。

## 23 传统/节日

**Q1: Why are traditions important?**
Traditions are important because they give people a sense of identity and belonging. They connect generations and create shared memories. For example, Spring Festival brings families together, even if people live far apart. Traditions also teach values like respect and gratitude. Of course, some traditions can be outdated, but many can adapt. So traditions are not just old habits; they are cultural glue.

**Q2: Should traditions change over time?**
Yes, I think they should. Traditions are living things, not museum pieces. For example, giving red envelopes digitally is a modern version of an old custom. The core value—sharing good wishes—stays the same. If traditions never change, they may become irrelevant. But if they change too much, they lose meaning. So the key is to keep the spirit while adapting the form.

**好的地方：** cultural glue, living things, core value, adapting the form。  
**背诵理解：** 传统题 = 身份归属 + 家庭连接 + 应变化 + 保留精神。

## 24 环境问题

**Q1: Who should solve environmental problems?**
I think it requires a collective effort. Governments should make laws and invest in clean energy. Companies should reduce waste and use sustainable materials. Individuals can change daily habits like using reusable bags. But individual action alone isn’t enough because big polluters have a much larger impact. So we need systemic change and personal responsibility together. Blaming only one group won’t solve the problem.

**Q2: Is individual action enough to protect the environment?**
No, but it’s still necessary. Individual action sends a signal to governments and businesses that people care. For example, if millions of people refuse single-use plastic, companies will notice. However, we also need policy changes, like carbon taxes and recycling systems. So individual action is a starting point, not the whole solution. It works best when combined with systemic change.

**好的地方：** collective effort, systemic change, sends a signal, carbon taxes。  
**背诵理解：** 环境题 = 多方责任 + 个人不够 + 政策 + 结合。

## 25 法律/规则

**Q1: Why do we need laws?**
We need laws to keep order, protect safety, and ensure fairness. Without laws, powerful people could do whatever they want, and vulnerable people would suffer. For example, the ban on smoking in public places protects non-smokers from second-hand smoke. Laws also set social expectations. Of course, laws must be just and enforced fairly. So laws are essential, but they need constant review.

**Q2: Are all laws fair?**
No, not all laws are fair. Some laws reflect the interests of powerful groups, and some become outdated. For example, historical laws once supported discrimination. That’s why people protest and push for legal reform. A fair legal system should allow debate, appeal, and change. So laws are necessary, but they are not automatically moral. Citizens should stay informed and hold leaders accountable.

**好的地方：** vulnerable, social expectations, outdated, hold accountable。  
**背诵理解：** 法律题 = 秩序安全 + 例子 + 不一定公平 + 改革与问责。

## 26 社会问题

**Q1: Why is loneliness increasing in big cities?**
Loneliness is increasing because cities are anonymous and people move around a lot. Many young people live alone and work long hours. Social media gives the illusion of connection, but it often lacks depth. For example, you can have hundreds of online friends and still feel disconnected. So urbanization and digital life both contribute. We need more community spaces and face-to-face interaction.

**Q2: How can communities reduce loneliness?**
Communities can reduce loneliness by creating easy ways for people to meet. That could be sports clubs, reading groups, volunteering, or neighborhood events. Public spaces like parks and libraries also help. For example, I joined a weekend hiking group and made friends. Governments can fund these activities, but individuals also need to take the first step. So connection requires both opportunity and effort.

**好的地方：** anonymous, illusion of connection, depth, take the first step。  
**背诵理解：** 孤独题 = 城市匿名 + 社媒浅层 + 社区活动 + 个人主动。

## 27 一个决定

**Q1: How do people make important decisions?**
People make important decisions in different ways. Some rely on logic, making pros and cons lists. Others follow intuition or ask family and friends. For example, when I chose my major, I talked to teachers and people in the field. Emotions also play a role, even when we think we are rational. So good decisions usually combine information, advice, and self-awareness.

**Q2: Should young people follow their parents’ advice?**
They should consider it, but not blindly follow it. Parents usually have more life experience and want the best for their children. But they may not understand new industries or the young person’s true interests. For example, my parents wanted me to study business, but I chose media because I was genuinely interested. So young people should listen, ask questions, and then decide for themselves. It’s their life.

**好的地方：** pros and cons, self-awareness, blindly follow, genuinely interested。  
**背诵理解：** 决定题 = 理性直觉 + 咨询 + 父母建议 + 自己负责。

## 28 一个建议

**Q1: What advice do young people need most?**
I think young people need advice on financial literacy, mental health, and career planning. Many leave school knowing how to pass exams but not how to budget, handle stress, or choose a path. They also need to learn that failure is normal. For example, the advice “start with ten minutes a day” helped me build habits. So practical, gentle advice is often more useful than big lectures.

**Q2: Is advice from the internet reliable?**
It’s mixed. The internet gives people access to a huge range of experiences and tips, which is great. But a lot of it is misleading, exaggerated, or designed to sell something. So people need critical thinking. They should check sources, compare different views, and be careful with advice that promises quick success. In the end, online advice can be a starting point, but personal judgment is essential.

**好的地方：** financial literacy, exaggerated, quick success, personal judgment。  
**背诵理解：** 建议题 = 年轻人需要 + 实用建议 + 网络真假 + 批判判断。

## 29 一次改变

**Q1: Why is change difficult for many people?**
Change is difficult because it pushes people out of their comfort zone. The unknown feels risky, and habits are hard to break. Social pressure also matters; people worry about what others will think. For example, moving to a new city was exciting but also lonely at first. However, change often leads to growth. So the difficulty is real, but it’s often worth it.

**Q2: How can people adapt to change?**
People can adapt by taking small steps, staying flexible, and asking for support. It also helps to focus on what they can control instead of what they can’t. For example, when I moved, I forced myself to join activities and talk to new people. A positive attitude and a routine make change less overwhelming. So adaptation is a skill, and like any skill, it can be practiced.

**好的地方：** comfort zone, unknown, overwhelming, adaptation is a skill。  
**背诵理解：** 改变题 = 困难原因 + 未知/习惯 + 小步/支持 + 适应是技能。

## 30 健康习惯

**Q1: Why are healthy habits hard to keep?**
Healthy habits are hard to keep because the rewards are long-term, while unhealthy choices feel good immediately. People are also busy, stressed, and surrounded by temptation. For example, it’s easier to scroll on the phone than go for a walk. Motivation alone isn’t enough; you need a routine and a supportive environment. So making habits small and easy is key. That’s why a 30-minute walk works better than a strict gym plan.

**Q2: Should governments promote healthy lifestyles?**
Yes, to some extent. Governments can fund public sports facilities, teach health education, and use taxes to discourage smoking or sugary drinks. But they shouldn’t become too controlling. People still need freedom to make personal choices. The best approach is to make healthy options easy and affordable. For example, safe parks and bike lanes encourage exercise without forcing anyone. So government should guide, not dictate.

**好的地方：** long-term rewards, temptation, supportive environment, guide not dictate。  
**背诵理解：** 健康题 = 短期诱惑 + 小习惯 + 政府推广 + 自由平衡。

# Part 3 练习方法

1. 每道题先自己说 45–60 秒，再对照范例。
2. 每道题只背：观点句 + 原因 + 例子 + 让步 + 总结。
3. 把范例改写成自己的经历和语气。
4. 录音回听，只抓：卡顿、重复词、语法错。
5. 30 篇 × 2 题 = 60 个 Part 3 高频追问，足够覆盖大多数抽象题。
